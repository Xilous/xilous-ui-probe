Eight ways to lay out the Settings page

Same content in every one — a theme picker and a live preview of the probe chrome. What differs is what gets the space, and what the layout is betting on.

These are live. Click any theme inside a prototype and that prototype re-themes. The palettes are the real eight from the plan.

True scale. The mock label and toolbar render at the extension's actual 11px, so nothing here is flattering itself.

Pick by the tradeoff line, not the prettiest render — every one of these can be made pretty. Tell me a number and I'll build it.

01

Sidebar list, large preview

The pattern people already know from editor preferences. Themes as a scannable vertical list on the left; everything else is preview.

Wins
Preview gets the most room of any layout here — the one where you can actually judge a palette. Instantly legible to anyone who has used VS Code's theme picker.
Costs
The sidebar is 218px of chrome dedicated to one setting. Looks under-furnished until a second setting exists.
Scales to
Many settings, if the sidebar becomes section nav rather than theme options — which is a rebuild, not a growth.
02

Self-previewing grid

No separate preview canvas at all — each card renders the chrome in its own theme, so the swatch is the preview and all eight are comparable in one glance.

chrome-extension://…/settings/index.html

Theme

Every card is a live render. Click to apply.

Wins
The only layout that answers "how do these compare?" rather than "what does this one look like?" — and comparison is the actual task. No dead space, no setting-of-one awkwardness.
Costs
Each render is small, so fine detail (shadow quality, border alpha, the muted greys) is hard to judge. Eight live chrome mocks is the heaviest DOM of the eight.
Scales to
More themes, gracefully. Other kinds of setting, awkwardly — a toggle has no card to render.
03

Pill bar, hero preview

Themes compress into one horizontal row so the preview can be centred and given the whole stage beneath it.

chrome-extension://…/settings/index.html

Theme

Applies to the probe UI on every site.

Wins
Cleanest hierarchy of the eight: one control, one result, nothing competing. Reads as a product page rather than a preferences dialog.
Costs
Nine pills in a row wrap unpredictably at narrow widths, and a pill can only carry a name plus a 20px swatch — less palette information than a list row.
Scales to
Poorly. A second setting has nowhere to go but under the pills, where it fights the preview for the same attention.

06

Simulated page, floating panel

The preview stops being a preview. The whole tab becomes a fake webpage with real probe chrome sitting on it — gear in the top-right and everything — and the picker floats over it like the tool does.

chrome-extension://…/settings/index.html

Theme

Chrome on the left is live. Gear is where it really sits.

Wins
The only one that shows the chrome in context — on a page, over content, at real scale, with the gear in its actual corner. It also previews the gear's top-right placement, which no other layout does.
Costs
Most work to build and the most to get wrong: a fake page convincing enough to judge against is its own design problem, and it dates. The floating panel overlaps the thing it's controlling — the exact collision §6 of the plan is about.
Scales to
Badly. A floating glass panel is not where anyone wants to manage five settings.
07

One at a time

A single theme fills the stage; arrows step through. Trades breadth of comparison for depth of attention on each palette.

chrome-extension://…/settings/index.html
Wins
Biggest single render, so subtle palette work — shadow tuning, border alpha, the muted grey ramp — is actually visible. Good keyboard story: arrows, and it's one clear focus target.
Costs
Nine themes behind a next button means most users see two of them. Choosing requires holding palettes in memory, which is the one thing a picker should spare you.
Scales to
Nothing else. It's a theme browser, not a settings page.
08

A/B compare

Optimised for the last step rather than the first: you've narrowed it to two and need to see them together to decide.

Wins
Side-by-side at full size is the only way to judge two close palettes — Dracula against Tokyo Night is genuinely hard otherwise. Explicit commit step, so nothing changes by accident.
Costs
Assumes you already have two finalists; there's no way to browse nine. Two dropdowns plus two commit buttons is the most interaction of any layout, for one preference.
Scales to
Nothing — but it composes. It's a good second screen behind layout 01 or 02.

Reading the eight together

Three of these are answering different questions. 02 is for comparing, 07 is for appreciating, 08 is for deciding between two. 01, 03 and 05 are the same layout at three levels of ambition. 04 is the only one designed for the page this becomes rather than the page it is, and 06 is the only one that shows the chrome where it actually lives.
