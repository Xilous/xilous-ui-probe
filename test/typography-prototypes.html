Twelve layouts, one question

Seven type controls in a 248px panel, without the wall of rows

The typography group now carries text, size, weight, leading, tracking, align and colour — seven rows, the tallest section in the panel. Each prototype below is the same state at true size; the faded spacing rows underneath show how much room each layout gives back. Click what looks clickable.

01

Paired columns

The four metrics come in natural pairs — size with weight, leading with tracking — so each pair shares a row with its own micro-labels. Nothing else changes dialect. Seven rows become five.

5 rows where there were 7. One dirty dot per pair covers both cells — the dot resets both, which is the honesty cost of sharing a row.

Wins
Cheapest possible change — same chips, same scrub, same everything, two rows shorter. Ships in an afternoon.
Costs
The dot column stops meaning "one property, one dot": a shared dot must reset a pair, or the pair needs two dots and the row gets crowded.
Scales to
Any group with natural pairs — border width/radius, shadow y/blur — but no further than pairing goes.

2 rows. The chip speaks the trade's own shorthand — 17/24 is how type specs have been written since paste-up. Point at a number and the caption names it.

Wins
The densest honest layout in the gallery, and it reads as a type spec rather than a form — the panel's most craft-literate moment.
Costs

Scales to
Exactly this group. Nothing else in the panel has a folk notation to borrow.

3 lines including the caption. The most radical reduction here — the values are the interface, chrome-free, like scrubbing the page itself.

Wins
Absolute minimum height and visual noise; for a returning user the row reads at a glance and scrubs without aiming at a chip.

Scales to
Power users, via a density preference; as the only layout it would cost more onboarding than it saves in pixels.

Wins

Costs

Scales to
Elements with short single-line text. For a paragraph the specimen degrades to an ordinary field with mood lighting.
12

Micro-ticker

Keep the exact current anatomy — dot, label, value, one per line — and shrink the line: 14px tall, 8px labels, values flush right, no chips until focus. Doing today's layout at two-thirds scale is itself an option, and the baseline the other eleven must beat.

Same seven rows at 14px each — the group drops from 154px to 104. No idea to learn, no dialect to defend; the question is only whether the smaller targets still scrub well.

Wins
A third of the height back with zero conceptual change — every behaviour, test and doc stays true. The safest possible ship.
Costs
Smaller hit targets across the board, and 8px labels flirt with the legibility floor on low-DPI screens; it spends the density budget without buying a better structure.
Scales to
The whole panel at once — and pairs with any of 01–11 later, since it changes sizes rather than shapes.

Reading order for a decision: 12 is the do-nothing-clever baseline; 01, 04 and 07 are today's dialect densified; 08 borrows the settings page's; 05 and 10 manage height by hiding; 02, 03, 06, 09 and 11 change what a control is. The strongest pairings: 07 for structure + 12's scale, or 02 as an adaptive-mode variant over an unchanged standard mode.
