Twelve architectures for the Edit panel

Same feature in every one — six groups (type, spacing, size, surface, border, shadow), token stepper beside raw value, dirty dots, one Copy that emits the delta block, a draggable header with the way back. What differs is the shape of the surface: how twenty controls organize into a floating panel, and what that shape costs.

These are live. Drag any value sideways to scrub it (or focus it and arrow-key, Shift for ×10), step tokens with ‹ ›, click swatches to cycle the page's palette. Dirty dots revert one property; Copy writes the real pointer + deltas block to your clipboard. Panels drag by their header. ‹ goes back to the toolbar; the pencil returns.

The card is honest. It is a real element being restyled inline — exactly what Edit Mode does. Same six groups, same both-mode controls in every frame; only the architecture changes. Theme dots in each title bar re-skin that frame.

Pick by the tradeoff line, not the prettiest render — every one of these can be made pretty. Tell me a number (or a hybrid) and I'll build it.

01

Inspector column

The Figma answer, verbatim. One narrow column, six titled sections, everything visible at the cost of height — the scrollbar is the tax, familiarity is the payoff.

https://skills.example.dev/catalog
Wins
Instant familiarity — anyone who has opened Figma or Sketch reads it at a glance, and every control keeps a permanent address, so muscle memory forms fast.
Costs
The tallest chrome of the twelve: over a short element it dwarfs the thing being edited, and the scroll hides whichever group you didn't park at.
Scales to
More groups gracefully — absorbing inventory is what inspectors do. Small viewports poorly.
02

Icon tab deck

Six groups, one visible. The panel stays business-card sized because it never shows two categories at once; the tab strip is the map of what's editable.

https://skills.example.dev/catalog
Wins
Smallest steady footprint that still carries the full inventory; dirty badges on the tabs say where you've been without opening anything.
Costs
Cross-group moves — radius and shadow together — become tab-hopping, and a group you never visit is a group you never discover.
Scales to
More groups by adding tabs, until the strip itself needs to scroll.
03

Accordion sheet

The settings page's collapsible sections, floated. Open what you're tuning, close what you're not — dirty dots keep the closed sections honest.

https://skills.example.dev/catalog
Wins
The user sets the height-versus-detail tradeoff per session, and the closed-section dots are a live preview of what the delta block will contain.
Costs
Two clicks to reach anything closed; open three sections and it's as tall as 01 with more furniture per group.
Scales to
Many groups gracefully — collapsing the long tail is the accordion's whole trick.
04

Spec ledger

The repo's spec-sheet dialect: label, leader dots, control. No section furniture at all — twenty rows under four quiet caps, maximum density, one dialect across the whole extension.

https://skills.example.dev/catalog
Wins
The most controls per pixel in the gallery, and it reads like the settings page — the extension keeps speaking one language everywhere.
Costs
Density is a skill issue: first-time users face a wall of rows, and the token chips crowd an already busy line.
Scales to
More properties by getting taller, never wider — and the wall only grows.
05

Toolbar that blooms

Edit Mode keeps the toolbar's shape: a horizontal strip of group buttons sits where the bar was, and the active group blooms beneath it. The mode transition is a morph, not a swap.

https://skills.example.dev/catalog
Wins
The seamless entry: the bar you clicked becomes the bar you tune with, in the same place — horizontal chrome that hugs the element's edge instead of covering the page beside it.
Costs
One group at a time, the bloom's height bounces as groups change, and a wide strip needs a wide viewport.
Scales to
Badly past about seven groups — the strip runs out of edge.
06

Console deck

All six groups side by side in one wide, short deck along the bottom — a mixing console under the page. Nothing hidden, nothing stacked, nothing covering the element.

https://skills.example.dev/catalog
Wins
Zero navigation — every control on screen at once, in a shape that never covers the element because it hugs the viewport's bottom edge.
Costs
Wide: it wants ~620px of viewport, and the columns force every control into its tersest, most cramped form.
Scales to
Wider viewports beautifully; phones and split screens not at all.
07

Chips and popovers

The panel is a reading of the current style — six value chips — and each chip opens the controls that change it. The summary is the interface.

https://skills.example.dev/catalog
Wins
Tiniest resting state in the gallery, and the chip row doubles as the delta summary — what you changed is literally what you're looking at.
Costs
Everything is two interactions deep, and popovers sprouting from a floating panel is chrome growing chrome.
Scales to
Lightly-tuned elements best; a heavy session reopens the same popovers over and over.
08

Style as a sentence

The element's style written as an editable sentence — every number scrubs in place, every token is a chip mid-prose. The settings gallery's "sentence" heritage, applied to the panel.

https://skills.example.dev/catalog
Wins
The most legible statement of current state — you read the element's style like documentation and edit it exactly where you read it.
Costs
Prose order is fixed, so finding "radius" means reading; and the scrub targets are small and shoulder to shoulder.
Scales to
The property set it was written for — every added property rewrites the sentence.
09

Essentials first

Six hero controls flat on the panel — size, color, fill, pad, radius, shadow — and a "more" pill per group for the long tail. The 80% loop pays nothing for the 20%.

https://skills.example.dev/catalog
Wins
The common loop is one interaction deep with zero hunting, and the panel stays short over any element.
Costs
The other twenty percent hides behind expanders that shift the layout, and any fixed hero pick is wrong for somebody.
Scales to
Long tails well — that's the point. A differently-shaped 80% badly.
10

Token ladders

Both-mode made spatial: raw values on the left, and on the right the design system's scales drawn as ladders — the value's position on the rail is the interface.

https://skills.example.dev/catalog
Wins
The token story is visible instead of named — you see 14 sitting on text-base and title-lg two rungs up; off-scale values are literally off the rail.
Costs
The widest interior of any stacked frame, and four ladders plus palette rails is a lot of ink for a handful of properties.
Scales to
Token-rich design systems superbly; token-less pages waste half the panel.
11

Fader bank

Every number is a fader with token detents. The panel is a tuning desk: grab anything, slide, feel the notches where the design system's stops are.

https://skills.example.dev/catalog
Wins
The most tune-feeling frame: the scrub affordance is explicit everywhere, and detents make tokens tactile rather than something you read.
Costs
Faders communicate position, not value — the numbers ride shotgun — and colors, alignment, and enums always need a second idiom.
Scales to
Numeric properties only; every non-number is an exception to the metaphor.
12

Text and box

Two tabs, one mental model: what the words look like, and what the box around them looks like — the box tab drawn as the box-model diagram itself, controls sitting where they act.

https://skills.example.dev/catalog
Wins
Matches how people describe elements — "the text is fine, the box is wrong" — and the diagram puts pad, size, and radius where they act instead of behind labels.
Costs
Six groups forced into two buckets means surface and shadow live in "box" by argument, and diagram controls carry no labels for newcomers.
Scales to
Exactly this inventory — the diagram has a finite number of edges to pin controls to.
