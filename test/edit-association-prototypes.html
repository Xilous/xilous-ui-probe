Twelve ways to say "this panel edits that element"

The selection box is a ring drawn 2px outside the element. In Edit Mode that ring sits on top of the exact pixels being tuned — border width, border colour, corner radius, shadow. You cannot judge a 2px stroke through an accent stroke. Every prototype below answers "which element" while leaving the element's border, corners and interior completely untouched.

Scrub the values. Drag any number chip sideways. Stroke, radius and shadow all really apply — watch whether the association gets in the way.

Quiet, then loud. Every one has a resting state and an amplified one. It amplifies on row hover, during a scrub, and for a beat after a value lands.

Drag the panel. Its header is a handle. The association has to survive the panel being anywhere.

Press "off-screen". Pushes the element toward the corner and out. The twelve answer this differently on purpose — that is part of the comparison.

Family · spanning the gap — a physical link between the two

01

Wire

One hairline curve from the panel to a dot resting in the gap outside the element. The canonical answer, and the one everybody pictures when they hear "connect them" — a physical line you can trace with a finger.

Wins
Unambiguous at any distance and in any direction. The terminal dot lands in dead space, so a 1px border and a 20px radius both stay perfectly readable underneath.
Costs
A curve crossing the page cuts through whatever content lies between, and on a busy page it reads as one more thing drawn on top. Needs a live rect: editing padding or width moves the anchor.
Off-screen
Clamps to the viewport edge and terminates in an arrowhead — the link never breaks, it just points.
Costs to build
Needs a refresh hook from the edit-apply path — there is no ResizeObserver in the codebase.
02

Elbow tether

The same link, routed orthogonally instead of curved. This is the vocabulary the product already speaks — computeRedline() is an axis-relation solver and the dashed guide is already a rendered primitive. Reads as a spec drawing rather than a UI flourish.

Wins
Continuous with Redline mode, so the tool gains no new visual language. Axis-aligned segments hide inside a page's own grid far better than a diagonal curve.
Costs
Two long runs cover more ground than one short curve, and the elbow can land on content in a way a bezier arcs over.
Off-screen
Hides outright. The elbow's whole legibility is knowing where both ends are.
Reuses
computeRedline() at content.js:1128, .ccp-redline-guide-h/v at content.css:137.
03

Leader and caption

The line stops short. It terminates at a small tag floating in nearby whitespace carrying the element's identity — an architectural callout. Nothing at all approaches the perimeter, and the association carries a name rather than just a direction.

Wins
The only spanning approach that also answers "which element is it" — div.skill-card is right there. Keeps a wide berth from the box.
Costs
Needs whitespace to put the caption in, and dense pages have none; the caption then either overlaps content or drifts far enough to be ambiguous.
Off-screen
The caption pins itself to the viewport edge and the leader drops — identity survives, geometry doesn't.
Note
Recovers the information the hushed #ccp-label stops showing in edit mode.

Family · pointing without spanning — direction, no line across the page

04

Gnomon

A tapered wedge on the panel's own border, aimed at the element and re-aiming as either moves. Nothing crosses the gap; the panel simply looks at what it owns. Zero pixels are drawn anywhere near the element.

Wins
The lowest-footprint approach that still has a direction. The page is never drawn on — not near the element, not between them. Immune to every rect problem, since it only needs a bearing.
Costs
A bearing is not an address. With two similar cards along the same ray it genuinely cannot disambiguate, and the user has to follow the aim by eye.
Off-screen
Keeps aiming — it is a compass, so leaving the viewport costs it nothing.
Scales to
Any distance. Degrades only with candidate density along the ray.
05

Traveling pulse

Nothing persistent whatsoever. On each value change a single dot runs from the panel to the element and dissolves into a ripple. The association is carried entirely by motion over time — the resting state is literally empty page.

Wins
The purest possible obedience to the constraint: at rest there is nothing on screen to obstruct anything. Ties the signal to consequence — you see the link exactly when you change something.
Costs
Glance away mid-session and there is no standing answer to "which one". Fails the user's own "quiet always" half outright, which is precisely why it's worth seeing.
Off-screen
No pulse fires — there is nowhere to send it.
Reduced motion
Falls back to a briefly-held chord between the two, not a removed animation — motion goes, information stays.

Family · marking the exterior — near the element, never on it

06

Corner brackets

Four L-shaped crop marks sitting outside the corners at a fixed offset, deliberately square — they do not follow border-radius. That refusal is the entire design: a mark that never traces the corner can never be confused with it.

Wins
Reads instantly as "this box" while touching none of it, and the square corners stay visually independent of the radius you're scrubbing — you can watch 0→24px with the marks completely still.
Costs
Four objects instead of one, and on a small element the brackets crowd until they nearly close into the ring we're trying to get away from.
Off-screen
Surviving corners stay; the clipped ones simply leave. Two brackets still bound the element usefully.
Costs to build
Live-rect dependent — needs the edit-apply refresh hook.
07

Edge ticks

Lighter than brackets: one short mark at the midpoint of each edge, floating in the exterior gap. Four points imply the box without describing it, and midpoints stay maximally far from the corners — the most delicate part of what's being edited.

Wins
The quietest of the exterior family. Deliberately puts its marks at the point on each edge furthest from any corner, so radius work is never visually crowded.
Costs
Weakest bounding read of the three exterior options — four ticks describe a cross, and the eye has to infer the rectangle.
Off-screen
Surviving ticks stay put; losing two of four degrades it badly.
Scales to
Large elements well, small ones poorly — the ticks converge.
08

Breathing offset ring · the control

A ring 8px out with a constant corner radius of its own, breathing slowly. This is today's box moved just far enough out to be legal — included on purpose as the baseline the other eleven have to beat.

Wins
Strongest possible bounding read, and no learning curve — it is the vocabulary users already have. The breath replaces the sweep as the "alive" signal at a fraction of the visual cost.
Costs
Its constant radius visibly disagrees with the element's own once you scrub radius past ~12px, which is either honest separation or an irritating mismatch. It also still fully encircles, so it competes with shadow work all round the box.
Off-screen
Clips. Half a ring is still a ring.
Reduced motion
Holds at higher opacity rather than losing the ring.

Family · remote — no proximity to the element at all

09

Gutter rails

Two bars pinned in the viewport's margins, spanning exactly the element's vertical and horizontal extent — a spreadsheet highlighting the row and column of the active cell. Association by projection, from as far away as it is possible to get.

Wins
Nothing is drawn within a hundred pixels of the element, and it still communicates position and size on both axes. Borrows a read every spreadsheet user already owns.
Costs
Indirect — you read two projections and intersect them mentally. Vertically stacked elements of equal width project identically.
Costs
The gutter is a strip we reserve but do not own. An element flush to the viewport edge slides under its own rail — the only approach here that can violate the constraint unaided. Each band has to relocate to the opposite margin to stay clear; press "off-screen" to watch both rails jump.
Off-screen
Degrades into a direction indicator for free: the rails compress toward the edge the element left through. The only approach that gets this without special-casing.
Scales to
Any element size. Struggles when the element spans nearly the full viewport.

Family · identity — matched objects instead of geometry

10

Shared tag

No connecting geometry at all. The panel header wears a small marked chip, and an identical chip floats just outside the element's top-left corner. Two matching objects; the eye pairs them the way it pairs a footnote with its marker.

Wins
Cheapest thing on the page — one chip in dead space above the corner. No path to route, no rect to trace, nothing that can collide with content between the two.
Costs
Requires a deliberate scan to find the matching chip; it never grabs you. And it marks one corner only, so it says "here" without saying "this big".
Off-screen
The panel chip is left alone with nothing to match — the weakest failure of the twelve.
Scales to
Multiple elements trivially, if that ever comes back — distinct glyphs, one chip each. (Explicitly not a requirement today.)

Family · panel-side only — nothing whatsoever drawn on the page

11

Live mirror

A live thumbnail of the element in the panel header, re-rendered on every change. The association isn't spatial at all — it's "what you see in the panel is what you're editing". The page is never touched by a single pixel.

Wins
The only approach that draws nothing on the page — perfect compliance with the constraint by construction, and the only one that still works with the element scrolled entirely out of view. Doubles as a before/after read at a glance.
Costs
Confirms identity but not location — with two similar cards it tells you what you're editing without telling you where. At thumbnail scale a 1px border change is nearly invisible, which is exactly the edit it most needs to show.
Off-screen
Entirely unaffected. This is its superpower.
Reuses
lib/html2canvas.min.js is already vendored for the screenshot action; the mock here clones and scales instead.

Family · depth — implied by elevation rather than marked

12

Lift

The element reads as raised off the page — an accent-tinted shadow cast into the gap around it by a caster sitting behind. Nothing traces it, nothing points at it; it is simply the only thing in the room that is off the ground.

Wins
Feels physical rather than annotated — the least "tool chrome" of the twelve. The glow occupies the gap outside the border without any hard edge for the eye to mistake for a stroke.
Costs
Collides head-on with the Shadow group, one of the six editable inventories. Tuning shadow-blur against a tinted glow of our own is genuinely confusing — the strongest single objection to any approach here.
Off-screen
Clips with the element.
Costs to build
Live-rect dependent, and needs a caster node behind the element in a page whose stacking contexts we don't control.
